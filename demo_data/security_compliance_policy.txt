# Enterprise Information Security & Compliance Policy Manual
Document ID: SEC-POL-2026-v3
Effective Date: January 1, 2026

## Section 1: Incident Reporting and Response Protocols
All suspected or confirmed security incidents must be reported to the Security Operations Center (SOC) within 60 minutes of initial detection. Suspected ransomware or active intrusion requires immediate host network isolation before opening the ticket. Unauthorized access to production databases holding customer records is automatically categorized as Severity 1 Critical, mandating immediate notification of legal counsel and executive leadership within 4 hours. Post-incident root cause analyses must be completed and documented within 10 business days.

## Section 2: Access Control, Authentication, and Session Management
All corporate user accounts require passwords of at least 14 characters, containing a combination of uppercase letters, lowercase letters, numbers, and special symbols. Phishing-resistant multi-factor authentication (MFA) via FIDO2 hardware tokens or authenticator push verification is mandatory for all remote VPN access, cloud management consoles, and privileged systems. Accounts are locked out after 5 consecutive failed login attempts and require administrator unlock or self-service identity verification. Administrative sessions must terminate automatically after 15 minutes of inactivity. Privileged and administrative access requests require dual authorization from both the system owner and the security officer, and all assigned access permissions are recertified quarterly by department managers.

## Section 3: Data Retention, Backup, and Media Sanitization
Access audit logs, system event logs, and firewall traffic records must be retained in write-once-read-many (WORM) immutable cloud storage for a minimum of 365 days. Mission-critical databases require daily full snapshots alongside continuous transaction logging, with backup replicas synchronized to a secondary cloud region located at least 250 miles away from the primary data center. Employee personnel files and termination records are archived securely for 7 years following employment separation. Physical storage media, including decommissioned solid-state drives and magnetic disks, must be degaussed and physically shredded by an accredited disposal vendor with a signed certificate of destruction.

## Section 4: Cryptographic Controls and Transmission Standards
All confidential data at rest stored in relational databases, object storage containers, or block volumes must be encrypted using AES-256 or approved cloud key management services. Master encryption keys must be configured for automatic rotation at least once every 12 months. All data in transit across public or untrusted networks must be encrypted using TLS 1.3 with forward secrecy cipher suites. Self-signed certificates are strictly prohibited in production; all public-facing and internal service endpoints must present digital certificates issued by an enterprise-approved Certificate Authority. Hash verification for integrity and code signing must use SHA-256, SHA-384, or SHA-512; legacy SHA-1 and MD5 hashes are prohibited.

## Section 5: Endpoint Device Compliance and Tamper Protection
All corporate workstations and laptops must enforce automatic screen locks after 5 minutes of inactivity. Corporate endpoint detection and response (EDR) software agents are tamper-protected and must never be disabled, uninstalled, or altered by end users. Operating system and third-party application patches for critical security vulnerabilities must be tested and rolled out within 7 calendar days of vendor release. Mobile device management (MDM) profiles enforce device encryption and automatically block and wipe corporate workspaces on rooted or jailbroken mobile devices. Personal USB flash drives and external storage devices are blocked by default on all company workstations. Lost or stolen corporate devices must be reported to the SOC within 2 hours to initiate immediate remote wipe commands.

## Section 6: Third-Party Risk Management and Vendor Assurance
All third-party vendors, suppliers, and contractors with access to corporate networks or data must complete a security risk evaluation. High-risk vendors processing sensitive data must undergo an annual security audit and provide an active SOC 2 Type II report or ISO 27001 certificate. All vendors must sign a Data Protection Addendum (DPA) committing to notify the company of any security breach within 24 hours of confirmation. External contractor and third-party vendor credentials must be configured with a mandatory expiration date aligned with the project completion date, after which accounts are automatically disabled by identity governance systems.
